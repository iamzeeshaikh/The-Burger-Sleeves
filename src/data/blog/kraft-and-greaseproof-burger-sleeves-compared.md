# Kraft vs Greaseproof Burger Sleeves Compared

Two sample sleeves arrive from the supplier on the same day, one warm brown and one bright white. Slide the same cooked burger into each and the differences start showing immediately. The kraft vs greaseproof burger sleeves question is really a question about your own menu. How much grease does the menu produce, how long do orders sit, and what should the band look like while doing its job. Both stocks succeed daily across thousands of American counters. This comparison lays out where each one honestly earns its place, so the order matches the food.

## Material construction differences

The two stocks come off different production lines with different goals. Construction explains everything that follows.

Kraft sleeve stock is unbleached paper or light board, dense and stiff from its long fibers. Its natural brown tone comes straight from the pulp, with no coating required for basic duty.

Greaseproof stock starts as paper refined until its fibers pack tightly enough to resist oil penetration on their own. Some grades add a treatment layer on top for heavier resistance. The sheet is usually thinner, more flexible, and translucent against strong light. Classic [kraft brown burger sleeves](/product/kraft-brown-burger-sleeves/) read as sturdy craft packaging in the hand. Greaseproof bands feel closer to a premium deli wrap that learned to hold a shape.

## Grease resistance in practice

Grease is the headline difference and the reason both stocks exist. The details are more interesting than the names suggest.

The names suggest a simple winner, and service reality is more even. Plain kraft absorbs grease, showing spots within a heavy meal. Greaseproof resists penetration far longer, keeping its face clean through the same burger.

Which behavior you want depends entirely on presentation philosophy. Grease spotting on kraft reads as authentic at smash burger counters and barbecue windows. The very same spotting reads as careless at polished fast-casual brands with white interiors. Purpose-treated [grease-resistant burger sleeve stock](/product/grease-resistant-burger-sleeves/) splits the difference, keeping the kraft look with an engineered barrier inside. For truly saucy builds, the barrier stops being optional regardless of the look you prefer.

## Appearance and customer feel

Customers meet the sleeve before the first bite, at hand and at eye. First impressions are stock decisions.

The two stocks tell different brand stories before a single word gets printed. Kraft signals natural, rustic, and handmade values. White greaseproof signals clean, precise, and chef-driven intent.

Neither story is wrong, and menus wear both well. Texture continues the story in the hand, where kraft feels toothy and greaseproof feels smooth.

The visual roles each stock plays sort into a familiar list:

- Kraft frames smash burgers, barbecue, and street food naturally
- White greaseproof suits chef-led and fast-casual counters
- Specialty [black burger sleeve formats](/product/black-burger-sleeves/) frame food dramatically for photos
- Kraft hides small scuffs that white surfaces show
- White reads cleaner under bright retail lighting
- Both take one-color logos handsomely at low cost

Match the sleeve tone to the room, the tray, and the photography style. The band appears in every customer photo, which makes this a marketing decision as much as a packaging one.

## Printing performance

Artwork meets the stock at the press, and the stock decides half the result. Knowing the behavior in advance saves proof rounds.

Print behaves differently across the two surfaces. Kraft's brown base tints every ink, muting cool colors toward earth tones. White greaseproof shows ink true, closer to printing on a blank page.

One and two color designs dominate sleeve printing on both stocks for good reason. Kraft flatters black, white, and warm reds while quietly swallowing pale blues. Greaseproof carries fine line work and small type more crisply across the band. Coverage-heavy designs sit better on greaseproof, where the ink lays down evenly on the smooth face. Brands wanting kraft's warm tone with accurate logo color often print a white underlayer first, at some added cost. Sampling both with your actual artwork answers the question in one proof round.

## Heat and moisture behavior

Temperature is the test kitchens forget to run. A sleeve that behaves at room temperature can act differently under a heat lamp.

Hot burgers steam from below and sweat at the band line. The two stocks respond differently across a long service window.

Kraft's absorbency works in its favor here, drinking small moisture without losing shape. Heavy steam eventually softens it, though the stiffer board grades resist the longest.

Cold weather adds one more wrinkle for outdoor service. A warm burger in freezing air condenses moisture fast at the band line. Kraft's thickness buffers that swing better than thin greaseproof sheet does. Greaseproof repels the moisture instead, staying intact while droplets sit on its face. Under heat lamps, both stocks perform reliably through normal holding times. Street service in cold weather favors kraft's insulating thickness by a small margin. Neither stock survives a genuinely wet serving tray, which is a service problem no sleeve on earth solves.

## Disposal considerations

Where the sleeve ends up after the meal now influences where the order goes before it. Buyers ask, and the answer should be ready.

Disposal questions now reach the packaging order sheet from customers and event organizers alike. The two stocks answer differently.

Plain kraft is the cleaner story, recycling with paper and composting in most US programs. Uncoated greaseproof grades compost as well, though heavily treated versions may not qualify. Grease loading complicates recycling for both stocks, since soiled paper belongs in compost, not recycling. Operators serving eco-minded events should confirm the exact grade's certification in writing before printing claims on the band. ## Choosing kraft vs greaseproof burger sleeves

The decision compresses into a short exercise once the tradeoffs are clear. Work through it with your menu list open.

1. Rank all your menu items from the driest to the greasiest build.
2. Time how long a typical order actually sits before eating begins.
3. Decide whether grease spotting fits or fights your brand look.
4. Check your artwork colors against both stocks in proofs.
5. Confirm disposal claims you plan to make at events.
6. Sample both stocks on your two most frequently photographed items.

Most operators land on kraft for dry-to-moderate menus and greaseproof for the saucy ones. Plenty of counters run both stocks at once, matching the sleeve to each item.
